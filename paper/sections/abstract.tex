\begin{abstract}
Agent-based models are used across epidemiology, ecology, economics and the social sciences,
yet studies still meet three obstacles: simulations too slow for the sweeps a careful study
needs, results that are hard to reproduce, and a gap between running a model and producing
numbers a reviewer can check. \modelly{} is a Python framework with a Mesa-compatible
programming model that addresses the three. It holds agent state in model-owned arrays
(columns) or counted populations (cohorts), runs its inner loops in C with pure-Python twins,
and ships the steps of a study: sweeps, calibration (ABC-SMC, simulated moments, gradients),
sensitivity analysis (Morris, Sobol, PAWN), emulators and history matching, data
assimilation, run manifests and bundles, and seventeen validated reference models. On eight
workloads written idiomatically for Mesa~2.3, Mesa~3.5 and \modelly{}, and four for
mesa-frames, the column form runs its steps 2.9 to 33 times faster than per-agent Mesa~3.5
code, 4.6 to 45 times faster than Mesa~2.3 and 3.0 to 9.4 times faster than mesa-frames, and
on three workloads 1.8 to 4.4 times slower than hand-written numpy without agent objects; the
cohort form of the one workload that has one runs 133 times faster than Mesa~3.5. Over 100
paired seeds, 63 of 67 summary statistics of a \modelly{} variant are equivalent to
Mesa~2.3's within half a standard deviation (29 after a Holm correction). Mesa models ported
without changing their logic, Mesa's own examples among them, run at 0.22 to 0.96 of Mesa's
CPU time, with bit-identical (Mesa~2.3) or equivalent (Mesa~3.5) output. Release 1.4 adds a run split over
MPI or Dask workers that reproduces the single-process run byte for byte, a GPU backend 34
times faster than the column form at ten million agents, and a model that runs in a browser
tab, each with what it does not yet do. \modelly{} is on PyPI as \code{modelly-abm}
under the Apache 2.0 licence.
\end{abstract}
