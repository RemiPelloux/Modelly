\begin{abstract}
Agent-based simulations are often too slow for the sweeps a study needs, hard to reproduce,
and hard for a reviewer to check. \modelly{} is an independent Python framework, written from
scratch, whose programming model is compatible with Mesa's. Its contribution is one
architecture: agents remain objects while their state can live in model-owned arrays
(columns), so that a model's population-wide phases can be rewritten as array operations one
at a time, or in counted populations (cohorts), with the steps of a study (sweeps,
calibration, sensitivity analysis, emulators, data assimilation, manifests, bundles) built on
the same seeding discipline. On eight workloads written idiomatically for each framework,
the column form runs its steps 2.9 to 33 times faster than per-agent Mesa~3.5 code (12 times
faster than Mesa~3.5 with a KD-tree on flocking), 3.0 to 9.6 times faster than mesa-frames,
and within 1.8 to 4.5 times of hand-written numpy without agent objects, its lead growing
with the population; an ablation that changes one thing per rung attributes the gain to the
array form of the phases rather than to the compiled runtime or the framework's calls;
Mesa~3.5 models migrated by a codemod, Mesa's own examples among them, run 1.05 to 2.3 times
faster unchanged. Whether the faster forms compute the same model is tested rather than
assumed: over 100 paired seeds, 63 of 67 summary statistics meet a prespecified equivalence
margin of half a standard deviation at nominal significance (29 after Holm correction; 48
within $\pm 5\%$ of Mesa's mean), and a trajectory-level study of three workloads, with
margins fixed in each quantity's own units, finds the faster forms' curves, spatial patterns
and rare events as close to Mesa~2.3 as Mesa~3.5's are. The reproducibility guarantees are
stated by scope and tested on nine hosted builds (Linux, macOS and Windows under CPython
3.12 to 3.14): twelve of seventeen examples give bit-identical digests everywhere and the
rest agree at ten significant digits wherever the suite compares them. \modelly{} also
splits a run across processes with byte-identical results, runs columns on a GPU (steps 34
times faster than one CPU core at ten million agents, 1.6 to 1.8 times once construction is
counted), differentiates models, assimilates live data and exports a model to a web page.
\end{abstract}
