\begin{abstract}
Agent-based simulations are often too slow for the sweeps a study needs, hard to reproduce,
and hard for a reviewer to check. \modelly{} is an independent Python framework, written from
scratch, whose programming model is compatible with Mesa's. Agents remain objects while their
state can live in model-owned arrays (columns), so that a model's population-wide phases can
be written as array operations one at a time, or in counted populations (cohorts); inner loops
run in C with pure-Python twins; and the steps of a study (sweeps, calibration, sensitivity
analysis, emulators, data assimilation, manifests, bundles) and seventeen tested reference
models are built in. On eight workloads written idiomatically for each framework, the column
form runs its steps 2.9 to 33 times faster than per-agent Mesa~3.5 code (12 times faster than
Mesa~3.5 with a KD-tree on flocking) and 3.0 to 9.6 times faster than mesa-frames (on its
four), and comes within 1.8 to 4.5 times of hand-written numpy without agent objects (on
three), its lead growing with the population; the cohort form of the one workload that has
one runs 135 times faster than Mesa~3.5. Over 100 paired seeds, 63 of 67 comparisons of a
statistic with Mesa are equivalent within half a standard deviation (29 with Holm-corrected
equivalence tests), and no difference survives Holm correction. Mesa~3.5 models migrated by a
codemod, Mesa's own examples among them, run 1.05 to 2.3 times faster on \modelly{}.
\modelly{} also splits a run across processes with byte-identical results, runs columns on a
GPU (steps 34 times faster than one CPU core at ten million agents), differentiates models,
assimilates live data and exports a model to a web page.
\end{abstract}
