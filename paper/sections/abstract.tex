\begin{abstract}
Agent-based simulations are often too slow for the sweeps a study needs, hard to reproduce,
and hard for a reviewer to check. \modelly{} is an independent Python framework, written from
scratch, whose programming model is compatible with Mesa's. Agents remain objects while their
state can live in model-owned arrays (columns) or counted populations (cohorts), so that a
model's population-wide phases can be rewritten as array operations one at a time, and the
steps of a study (sweeps, calibration, sensitivity analysis, emulators, data assimilation,
provenance) share one seeding discipline. On eight workloads written idiomatically for each
framework, the column form runs its steps 2.9 to 33 times faster than per-agent Mesa~3.5 code
and within 1.8 to 4.5 times of hand-written numpy without agent objects, and an ablation
attributes the gain to the array form of the phases. Mesa~3.5 models migrated with a codemod
(Mesa's own examples with one import edit each) run 1.05 to 2.3 times faster. Against the author's Mesa~2.3
reference implementations, 63 of 67 summary statistics over 100 paired seeds meet a
prespecified equivalence margin of half a standard deviation, and a trajectory-level study
finds the faster forms as close to Mesa~2.3 as Mesa~3.5 is. The released wheels, installed
from PyPI on five architectures under CPython 3.12 to 3.14, give bit-identical digests for
thirteen of seventeen examples and agree to ten significant digits for the rest. \modelly{}
also splits a run across processes with byte-identical results, runs columns on a GPU,
differentiates models, assimilates live data and exports a model to a web page.
\end{abstract}
