\begin{abstract}
Agent-based models are used across epidemiology, ecology, economics and the social sciences,
yet studies still meet three obstacles: simulations too slow for the sweeps a careful study
needs, results that are hard to reproduce, and a gap between running a model and producing
numbers a reviewer can check. \modelly{} is an open-source Python framework with a
Mesa-compatible programming model that addresses the three. It adds agent state held in
model-owned arrays (columns), counted populations (cohorts), compiled inner loops with
pure-Python twins and reproducible random streams, and it ships the steps of a study: parameter
sweeps, calibration (ABC-SMC, simulated moments), global sensitivity analysis (Morris, Sobol,
PAWN), run manifests and archivable bundles, and twelve reference models with validation tests.
On eight workloads written idiomatically for Mesa~2.3, for the current Mesa~3.5 and for
\modelly{}, and on four of them for the vectorised mesa-frames, vectorised \modelly{} code (the
column form) runs the simulation steps 3.2 to 31 times faster than per-agent Mesa~3.5 code, 5.0
to 45 times faster than Mesa~2.3 and 3.0 to 9.4 times faster than mesa-frames, and 1.8 to 4.6
times slower than hand-written numpy without agent objects; on the one workload with a cohort
form, that form runs 133 times faster than Mesa~3.5. Over 100 paired seeds, 63 of 67
comparisons of a summary statistic between a \modelly{} variant and Mesa~2.3 are equivalent
within half a standard deviation at the 5\% level (29 after a Holm correction), and every
difference of means is at most 11\%. Mesa models ported without changing their logic run at
0.22 to 0.99 of Mesa's CPU time, depending on how much of it the framework's calls take,
with bit-identical output for three Mesa~2.3 models and equivalent distributions for five
Mesa~3.5 ones. \modelly{} is on PyPI
as \code{modelly-abm} under the Apache 2.0 licence.
\end{abstract}
