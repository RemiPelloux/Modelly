\begin{abstract}
Agent-based simulations are often too slow for the sweeps a study needs, hard to reproduce,
and far from numbers a reviewer can check.
\modelly{} is a Python framework with a Mesa-compatible programming model that addresses the
three: agent state in model-owned arrays (columns) or counted populations (cohorts), inner
loops in C with pure-Python twins, and the steps of a study (sweeps, calibration, sensitivity
analysis, emulators, data assimilation, manifests, bundles) and seventeen validated reference
models. On eight workloads written idiomatically for each framework, the column
form runs its steps 2.9 to 33 times faster than per-agent Mesa~3.5 code, 4.6 to 45 times
faster than Mesa~2.3 and 3.0 to 9.4 times faster than mesa-frames (on its four), and 1.8 to
4.4 times slower than hand-written numpy without agent objects (on three); the cohort form of
the one workload that has one runs 133 times faster than Mesa~3.5. Over 100 paired seeds, 63
of 67 summary statistics of a \modelly{} variant are equivalent to Mesa~2.3's within half a
standard deviation (29 after Holm correction), with no mean differing by more than 11\%.
Mesa models ported without changing their logic, Mesa's own examples among them, run between
Mesa's speed and 4.6 times faster (0.22 to 1.02 of its CPU time). \modelly{} also splits a
run across processes byte-identically, runs columns on a GPU (34 times faster at ten million
agents), differentiates models, assimilates live data and exports a model to the browser, each
with its stated shortfalls.
\end{abstract}
