\section{When to use \modelly{}}
\label{sec:usecases}

\modelly{} is most useful when a study needs many runs, large populations, or results that
others must be able to check. \Cref{tab:usecases} summarises the situations where it helps
most, and the part of the framework that does the work.

Some examples by field: an epidemiologist calibrating an age-structured transmission model to
case counts, then ranking intervention parameters by Sobol index; an ecologist running a
stage-structured insect model with cohorts over a factorial of release strategies; an
economist studying wealth inequality across a Latin hypercube of exchange rules on a cluster;
a social scientist comparing opinion dynamics on household, school and work networks; a
transport researcher routing commuters on a city's road graph. In each case the model is
ordinary Python, and the study's runs, fit and sensitivity analysis share one seed and one
record.
