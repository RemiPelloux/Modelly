Agent-based simulations are often too slow for the sweeps a study needs, hard to reproduce, and hard for a reviewer to check. Modelly is an independent Python framework, written from scratch, whose programming model is compatible with Mesa's. Agents remain objects while their state can live in model-owned arrays (columns) or counted populations (cohorts), so that a model's population-wide phases can be rewritten as array operations one at a time, and the steps of a study (sweeps, calibration, sensitivity analysis, emulators, data assimilation, provenance) share one seeding discipline. On eight workloads written idiomatically for each framework, the column form runs its steps 2.9 to 33 times faster than per-agent Mesa 3.5 code (12 times on flocking against Mesa 3.5 given the same KD-tree search) and within 1.8 to 4.5 times of hand-written numpy without agent objects, and an ablation attributes the gain to the array form of the phases. Mesa 3.5 models migrated with a codemod run 1.05 to 2.3 times faster (1.05 to 1.7 on eight of Mesa's own ten examples). Against the author's Mesa 2.3 reference implementations, over 100 paired seeds, 29 of 67 statistic–variant comparisons are shown equivalent within half a standard deviation after Holm correction (63 at nominal significance), and no difference survives Holm correction, and a trajectory-level study finds the faster forms as close to Mesa 2.3 as Mesa 3.5 is on the dynamics tested, with one timing difference shown. The released wheels, installed from PyPI on five architectures under CPython 3.12 to 3.14, give bit-identical digests for thirteen of seventeen examples and agree to ten significant digits for the rest wherever the suite compares them. Modelly also splits a run across processes with byte-identical results, runs columns on a GPU, differentiates models, assimilates live data and exports a model to a web page.
